How a build runs

Four weeks, in order. No discovery theatre.

Each phase ends in something you can look at — a written runbook, a working pilot, a handover doc. If week one says automation is the wrong answer, we tell you and stop there.

Week 1

Shadow

We sit in your tools and watch a real week. Every repeated action gets timed and counted. You get the tally, ranked by hours and by how badly it breaks when it slips.

Week 2

Runbook

The top workflow written out as rules a person could follow: triggers, edge cases, tone, who owns the exceptions. You approve it in plain English before anything gets built.

Week 3

Pilot

Built and run in shadow mode — it drafts, you send. We compare its calls against yours until the gap is boring, then take the training wheels off one rule at a time.

Week 4+

Handover

Runbooks and code land in your workspace. We stay on watch: the shift log every morning, a monthly tune, and someone on call when a vendor changes an API at 3am.

Human-in-the-loop

It never quietly guesses on your behalf.

Every action is scored before it runs. The threshold is yours to set, per workflow, and you can move it after a fortnight of watching it work.

Straight answers

The questions that come up on every first call.

Where does our data go?

It stays in your accounts. We build inside your Google Workspace, your CRM, your books package, using credentials you issue and can revoke in a click. Nothing is copied into a warehouse of ours, and nothing is used to train anything. The model providers we call are on a written subprocessor list you get before signing, all on zero-retention terms.

What happens when it breaks at 3am?

Every workflow has a kill switch that any of your staff can hit, and a failure mode that stops rather than improvises. Overnight breakages page us, not you. You'll read about it in the morning brief with the cause and the fix — and, if it sent something wrong, exactly who received it.

Do we own what you build?

Yes. Code, runbooks and prompts are yours from day one and sit in your own repository and workspace. If you leave, you keep a working system and a document that explains it. No black boxes, no licence that expires with the retainer.

We already have someone technical. Do we need you?

Often not for the building — but usually for the first two weeks. The hard part isn't the code, it's writing down what your team actually does and where the judgement calls are. Plenty of clients take the runbook from week two and build it themselves. We'll say so on the call if that's the honest answer.

Which tools do you work with?

Google Workspace and Microsoft 365, HubSpot, Pipedrive, Airtable, Notion, Jobber, ServiceTitan, QuickBooks, Xero, Stripe, Slack, WhatsApp Business, Calendly and anything with a documented API. If your core system has none, we'll tell you in week one rather than three months in.
